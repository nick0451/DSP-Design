F97 DERIVED: S12 10 mV read as a broadband peak permits a 65 dB SPL pop

The operator said plainly they did not have the knowledge to set this, which is
a delegation, so it is derived here rather than asked for a third time. Every
input stated; none taken from S12.

Audibility at shutdown: click threshold in quiet 36.4 dB SPL for 100 us
(Stapells JASA 72(1):74, 1982), temporal integration 3 dB per halving (Moore,
Springer Handbook 13.1) puts a 1 ms event at 26.4 dB, and masking from 42 dBA
broadband inside a 365 Hz critical band gives 24.6 dB. Masking dominates at ms
scale, so the audible threshold is about 30 dB SPL.

Calibration against industry: TI 7 mV typ at the speaker is 40.9 dB SPL at 1 m,
which is ABOVE that threshold. Industry-typical pop is faintly audible in a
silent car, so no audible pop is stricter in kind than any vendor guarantees.

Referred back through a 10 dB amp to our RCA output, with 12 channels power
summing: inaudible is 0.183 mV, industry parity is 0.639 mV.

And S12 as written, through the same chain: 10 mV at RCA becomes 32 mV per
channel at the speaker, 54 dB SPL per channel, 65 dB SPL total. That is the
level of quiet speech. S12 permits a pop you could hold a conversation over -
about 50x in voltage looser than inaudible.

The reconciliation is why TI uses a MASK: a 10 mV DC step and a 10 mV fast click
are not the same event. A slow step puts almost no energy in the audio band.
That is why TI allows 10.7 mV at 100 Hz and 0.407 mV at 2.63 kHz. S12 10 mV is
defensible as a DC-step limit and indefensible as a broadband peak, and as
written it does not say which it is.

Proposed two-part criterion: (i) DC step <= 10 mV, keeping the number and citing
SLEA044 p.12; (ii) weighted peak <= 0.2 mV, 20Hz-20kHz AES17 brickwall, ITU-R
BS.468-4 renormalised to 2 kHz, quasi-peak detector, silent input - both
referred to a stated 10 dB downstream amp gain. Two parts because one number
cannot cover both failures, and S12 already learned this once: the CCIF
criterion exists because 1 kHz and 20 kHz THD are blind to nonlinear
off-capacitance. A DC-step limit is blind the same way to the same part.

Warning recorded with it: 0.2 mV is ~50x tighter than the current text and may
be expensive. That is not a reason to soften it here. If F98 finds it
infeasible the operator should relax the REQUIREMENT knowingly, not pick a
number because it is achievable. A criterion chosen to match what passes is the
failure F97 was opened to prevent.

F99 still comes first.
