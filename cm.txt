D-5: S7.3 steps swapped. F99 CLOSED. Cascade traced - one item does NOT dissolve.

Operator decree: soft-mute the DACs first, then engage the hard mute into
digital silence. S7.4 unchanged.

What cascades:
  F100 DISSOLVES. The crossing was pop <= 657 ohm against THD >= ~850 ohm. The
  657 upper bound came from the orderly-path transient, which no longer exists,
  so the constraints stop crossing. No feasible value was a property of the
  sequence, not of the board.
  V-16 SETTLED. DN2540 decreed, so F89 pin-incompatibility no longer blocks a
  footprint - only one candidate remains. TBD_V15 can be drawn: 12 of the 42
  outstanding footprints. F90 still applies - singles in pairs means
  MUTE_DEPFET needs 8 pins, not 3.

What does NOT, and this is where I must not overclaim:
  F98 is RE-SCOPED, not deleted. The shunt keeps two jobs the reorder cannot
  touch - steady-state C_off leakage, and the COLLAPSE path. S7.4 has no MCU
  and no soft-mute: rails fall, the DAC dies, the series relay opens in <=0.4 ms,
  and the shunt does not conduct until 33-75 ms (F108, tau = 50 ms). Swapping
  steps 2 and 3 does nothing there because neither step runs.

So the reorder fixes the ORDERLY pop, which is what S12 measures, and leaves the
DISORDERLY one exactly where F108 found it. Two paths, two problems; the decree
solved one.

That is the right split though, and it simplifies the argument: S12 is an
acceptance criterion testing the orderly transition, S7.4 is a fault-tolerance
requirement and not an acceptance test. They were being sized against a single
number and should never have been - that conflation is what produced F100 phantom
crossing.

F95 is now the gating question and the last one: no mute-DEPTH figure in dB
exists anywhere. S12 has a noise floor and now a transient pair; neither is an
attenuation spec. Once the operator states which question the mute answers, F98
is arithmetic.

Three decrees now sit in STATE and not in REQUIREMENTS - D-1 (S3 oscillator),
D-4 (S12 criterion), D-5 (S7.3 order). Worth one editing pass rather than three.
